KIIP vs TOPIK for F-2-7 points

By Kevin Herrbach · Updated 2026-10-21

Korean is the only F-2-7 category you can move in months rather than years. Age is fixed, your degree is done, and income moves once a year. Language is where a short plan pays.

Both routes land on the same scale:

Level TOPIK KIIP F-2-7 points
1 TOPIK 1 Stage 1 completed 3
2 TOPIK 2 Stage 2 completed 5
3 TOPIK 3 Stage 3 completed 10
4 TOPIK 4 Stage 4 completed 15
5 TOPIK 5 or 6 Stage 5 completed 20

One line is not on that table and it decides the comparison: completing KIIP stage 5 also gives a 10 point bonus. TOPIK 6 is 20 points. KIIP 5 is 30.

What KIIP actually is

KIIP, the 사회통합프로그램, is the Ministry of Justice's own integration course, run through socinet.go.kr. Six stages:

Stage Hours Ends with
0 15 nothing, it is an intro
1 to 4 100 each stage 4 ends with the 중간평가 (mid-term test)
5 basic 70 the 종합평가 (comprehensive test), pass mark 60 out of 100
5 advanced 30 needed for naturalization, not for the F-2-7

You do not start at stage 1. You sit a placement test, the 사전평가, and your score puts you in:

사전평가 score Placed in stage
0 to 2 0
3 to 20 1
21 to 40 2
41 to 60 3
61 to 80 4
81 to 100 5

So someone who already speaks decent Korean can score 81 on the placement test, enter stage 5 directly, sit 70 hours of class, pass the 종합평가, and collect 30 points. Realistic timeline: one placement test, one term of evening or weekend classes, one exam. Four to six months if the dates line up.

Points come from completing a stage, meaning the attendance and the test. Being placed in a stage by the 사전평가 is not completion. Ask your immigration office before counting placement alone.

Classes are two or three evenings a week or a Saturday block, at a 거점기관 near you: universities, multicultural centers, some churches. Since 2025 stages 1 to 4 carry a per-stage fee; check socinet for the current amount. The national test calendar for 2026 lists 8 placement tests, 4 mid-term tests and 8 comprehensive tests, so a missed date costs you one to two months.

What TOPIK is

A written exam, six times a year in Korea, run by the National Institute for International Education. TOPIK II covers levels 3 to 6 in one sitting. Results take about a month. The certificate is valid for 2 years, after which it is worth zero points.

No classes, no attendance, no fixed schedule. Register, study on your own terms, sit the exam. If you fail to reach the level you need, the next sitting is roughly two months away.

Which one, by situation

  • You are already around level 3 to 4 and need 20 or more points: KIIP. Place into stage 4 or 5, finish, take the 20 plus the 10 bonus. Nothing on the TOPIK side comes close to 30.
  • You need 5 to 10 points by a fixed date in under three months: TOPIK. One sitting, one month for results. KIIP cannot be compressed below a term.
  • You are a beginner: neither moves fast. TOPIK I gets you 3 to 5 points in one sitting. KIIP stage 1 to 2 takes 200 class hours for the same 5 points, but you are building toward the stage 5 bonus and toward permanent residency, which now requires KIIP anyway.
  • You are thinking past the F-2-7: KIIP. The F-5-16 permanent residency route requires KIIP stage 5 or a 60 on the 종합평가. TOPIK 4 used to be accepted for that and no longer is.

The expiry trap

TOPIK expires 2 years after the test date. Plan it so the certificate is valid on the day you file, and also on the day you renew, which is 1 to 5 years later. KIIP completion does not expire.

My own position

I got my first F-2-7 and one renewal with zero language points, on age, a STEM master's and income. I am in KIIP stage 3 now. That is 10 points once the stage is done, 30 if I carry on to stage 5, and it removes the need to clear 80 on income alone every renewal.
