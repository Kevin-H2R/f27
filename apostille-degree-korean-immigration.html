Apostille and translation of a foreign degree for Korean immigration

By Kevin Herrbach · Updated 2026-10-21

Education is worth up to 25 F-2-7 points, and the diploma is the one document you cannot get from a Korean website. Get this right once, and keep the result.

What immigration wants

A foreign degree certificate that a Korean officer can trust, which means one of:

  • Apostille, if your country is in the Hague Apostille Convention. A stamp or attached page from a designated authority in the country that issued the degree. Korea joined in 2007, most of Europe, the US, Australia and Japan are members.
  • Consular legalization, if your country is not a member. The document is certified in the issuing country, then stamped by the Korean embassy there. China and Canada are the common cases.

The apostille must come from the country of the university, not the country you live in. A French diploma gets a French apostille even if you are in Korea.

Plus a translation into Korean or English if the degree is in another language. Korean offices accept English. A French, German or Spanish diploma needs translating; an English one does not.

Who issues the apostille

It depends on the country and sometimes on the type of document. The usual pattern:

Country Where
France Cour d'appel of the region where the university sits; since 2025 notaries can issue them too
Germany Varies by state; usually the Regierungspräsidium or Landgericht
USA Secretary of State of the state where the university is
UK FCDO Legalisation Office, by post
Australia DFAT

Most of them want either the original diploma or a certified copy made by a notary, and some want the university to confirm the document first. Budget 2 to 6 weeks and a courier each way if you are doing it from Korea. A friend or family member at home with power of attorney is faster.

Translation

Three routes, from cheapest to most accepted:

  1. Translate it yourself and sign a statement that it is accurate. Some offices accept this for English translations of short documents. Risky for a diploma.
  2. Translation agency with a company seal. Most Korean immigration offices accept this.
  3. 번역공증, a notarized translation from a Korean notary (공증사무소). Costs 30,000 to 60,000 won per page, done in a day. This is the version nobody questions.

Immigration guides note that the translation is treated as valid for 3 months from its date, so do it close to the appointment, not next to the apostille.

What to bring to the counter

  • The apostilled original, or the consular-legalized original.
  • A photocopy of it for the file.
  • The translation, stapled to a copy of the diploma.

Do not hand over your only original

When I first came to Korea on an E-7, immigration kept my apostilled original diploma. Years later, at my F-2-7 appointment, all I had was a photocopy, and the officer rightly asked for an original. She accepted it in the end. It could have gone the other way, and a replacement apostille from France would have meant months.

Two ways to avoid this:

  • Order two apostilled copies before you leave home. Universities issue duplicate certificates or certified copies, and the apostille authority will stamp both.
  • At the counter, say you need the original back and offer the photocopy for the file. Officers do this routinely when asked.

If you already lost it

Ask the university for a certified duplicate or an official certificate of graduation. Get that apostilled. For the F-2-7, some offices accept a university-issued graduation certificate sent directly to them, or a 학력 verification through a service such as the Korean Council for University Education's foreign degree verification. Call 1345 and ask your office what they take before paying anyone.
