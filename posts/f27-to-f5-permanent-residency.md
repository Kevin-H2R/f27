# From F-2-7 to F-5 permanent residency

The F-2-7 is designed as a staging visa. Hold it long enough and keep your points up, and the next step is the F-5-16, points-based permanent residency, which removes renewals, points recounts and employer dependence for good.

## The requirements

As of the 2026 guidelines:

1. **Three years continuously on the F-2-7** up to the filing date. Years on E-7 or D-visas before it do not count toward this clock.
2. **80 points or more** on the same points table used for the F-2-7, recounted at filing.
3. **Income**: your own previous-year income at or above **twice the GNI per capita**, roughly 105 million won in 2026, or net assets above a set multiple of the national average. Only the applicant's income counts; a spouse's does not.
4. **Korean integration**: KIIP stage 5 completed, or a pass (60 or more) on the KIIP 종합평가. TOPIK no longer substitutes for new applicants.
5. **Clean record**: no serious convictions, no work in entertainment or other restricted sectors for 3 years before filing, no false documents.

The income line is the hard one. Most F-2-7 holders clear 80 points comfortably by year three; far fewer clear twice GNI on their own salary. Check the current figure on HiKorea each year, as GNI moves.

## Other F-5 routes you might qualify for sooner

- **F-5-1, general**: 5 years of legal stay on any long-term status, income above GNI per capita, and KIIP stage 5. The clock is longer but counts your E-7 years, and the income bar is half the F-5-16 one.
- **F-5-10, advanced degree**: a Korean master's or doctorate, or a foreign doctorate in a listed field, plus employment and income conditions.
- **F-5-2, marriage**: spouse of a Korean national, 2 years on F-6.

If you have been in Korea longer than five years in total, compare F-5-1 against F-5-16 before assuming the points route is faster.

## Planning the three years

Year one on the F-2-7:
- Register on 1365 or VMS and log volunteer hours. By year three that is +5 to +7 bonus points and evidence of integration.
- Sit the KIIP placement test. Aim to be in stage 4 or 5 by the end of year two.

Year two:
- Finish KIIP stage 5 and pass the 종합평가. This is a hard requirement for the F-5-16 and worth 30 F-2-7 points along the way, which lets your F-2-7 renewal come with a longer stay period.
- Push income into the bracket you need. The threshold is on taxable income, so negotiate salary rather than allowances that are excluded from the 소득금액증명.

Year three:
- File the F-5-16 as soon as last year's 소득금액증명 exists, 1 May for salaried people, 1 July for 종합소득세 filers, and the three-year date has passed.

## Documents

Expect the F-2-7 file again, plus the integration and income proofs: 통합신청서, passport, ARC, photo, 점수계산표 with proofs, 소득금액증명 and 원천징수영수증 or tax return, employment contract and employer registration, proof of address, KIIP certificate, overseas criminal record from your home country with apostille if you have not provided one within the last few years, and the 1365 or VMS certificate if you claim volunteer points. Fee around 200,000 won plus the new card.

## What the F-5 changes

No more extensions, no more points recounts, no employer tie, voting in local elections after three years, and your ARC becomes a permanent residence card renewed every 10 years. You still need re-entry rules in mind: more than two years outside Korea without a re-entry permit ends the status.
